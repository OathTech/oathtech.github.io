A Focused Research Organization working on AI oversight via formal methods.

About

Oath Technologies is building tools for overseeing advanced AIs. Most of our work is based on the Lean proof assistant.

As AIs become more capable, we expect they will become harder for humans to oversee. This is because they will act faster than we can monitor and build bigger than we can comprehend.

We think formal verification can help scale human understanding and trust. The hardest part of oversight will be specifying what humans want in a way that is understandable and robust to AI deception.

We are particularly interested in using AI to build:

  • Tools that bridge the gap between other languages and Lean, such as our cerberus-lean, golean, and ACL2Lean.
  • Models of real-world systems, such as our lean-ux-proto model of selected Linux kernel subsystems.
  • Applications of formal tools to critical systems, such as our work on finding power-grid bugs for tens of dollars.

Oath is a high-risk research organization trying to push the frontiers of possibility. Our aim is to keep humans safe and in control as we move through these extraordinary times.

Tools

Disclaimer: most of our tools are research prototypes. Please do not use them in production unless you know what you are doing.

  • Oct 2026
    lean-ux-proto — an executable Lean 4 model of selected Linux subsystems, tested against an instrumented Linux kernel.
  • Aug 2026
    brick-wp — a tactic library for verifying C++ with BRiCk.
  • Aug 2026
    grossmith — a Go program generator for differential testing of Go reimplementations and semantics.
  • Aug 2026
    cerberus-lean — a port of the Cerberus C semantics to Lean 4, differentially validated against the OCaml implementation.
  • Jul 2026
    covmap — a tool that keeps specifications connected to the code that implements them.
  • Jun 2026
    golean — an executable Go semantics in Lean 4, validated by differential testing.
  • Mar 2026
    ACL2Lean — a toolchain that replays ACL2 proofs as kernel-checked Lean 4 proofs.
  • Feb 2026
    lean-tcb — a trusted-computing-base analyzer for Lean 4: the definitions you need to review to trust a theorem.
